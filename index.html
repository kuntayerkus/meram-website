One key is
the whole interface.

There is nothing to switch to and nothing to open. Hold the shortcut in any application and a two-hundred-pixel capsule appears over whatever you were already doing.

Hold Ctrl+Shift+Space

Speak, and it
comes alive.

Three curtains of light, driven by four frequency bands read straight off the microphone, and a deep click the moment the stream opens. You never have to wonder whether it heard you.

The thread beside this text runs the app’s own code — peak-hold with an exponential release, three springs, sixty-four samples a frame.

Then it cleans up
after you.

The restarts and the “um”s go. Your own words — names, jargon, the spelling you actually use — come out of a dictionary that never leaves your machine. The capsule turns to paper while it works, so you can read the state without hearing a sound.

It lands where you
already were.

One sweep of light confirms it, and the text is typed into the field you left — not into a window you now have to copy out of and paste back.

Unless it can’t
prove that’s you.

Before a single keystroke, Meram compares the window, the process, when that process started, and which control has focus.

  • exactSame window, same field. Typed straight in.
  • window_onlyThe window matches, the field can’t be proven. Clipboard, and the capsule offers it back.
  • refusedPassword field, elevated window, Secure Input. Nothing is typed and nothing is copied.

Target lock

It would rather not type
than type in the wrong window.

Every commit is checked against the window handle, the process id, the moment that process started, and the control that currently has focus. The comparison returns a confidence rather than a yes or no — and “the window matches but the field doesn’t” is treated as a failure, because it is one.

Windows reports when a target is elevated and macOS reports when Secure Event Input is active. Both are detected before a keystroke is attempted, not discovered afterwards. A password field receives nothing at all — not even a copy on your clipboard.

Clipboard fallback

A dictation you can’t get back
is worse than no dictation.

When the text can’t be typed, it goes to your clipboard and the capsule grows a Copy button instead of an apology. Finish the thought, look up, and the words are still there — in the field, or one keystroke away from it.

You can also end a recording with the mouse rather than the keyboard, which matters more than it sounds like it should when the hand you’d reach with is already holding something else.

The audio chain

Noise suppression is off.
On purpose.

WebRTC noise suppression is tuned for human listeners, and it eats the fricatives — ş, ç, s, h, f — that carry meaning in Turkish. Switching it on makes a recording sound cleaner and makes the transcript worse. So it is off, and this is the note that says so instead of a settings toggle nobody finds.

There is exactly one gain stage — the browser’s own AGC — and behind it a limiter rather than a compressor. A compressor rides the whole signal and lifts the room between words; the gaps stop reading as silence, and Whisper invents text to fill them. Whisper mode applies a real 2.5× lift when you speak quietly.

Who this is for

People who write in Turkish

The audio chain and the vocabulary are tuned for Turkish rather than adapted to it afterwards. Say it in Turkish and land it in English if you want to — translation is one setting, not a second application.

Developers

Your tools come out spelled correctly. Terms are mined from your own history and handed to the model as biasing before transcription, which works far better than correcting the same word every day.

Anyone who answers a lot of messages

Meram knows which application has focus and can match the register to it, so the same sentence lands looser in a chat than in a document without you thinking about it.

Refinement

Not a transcript.
What you meant.

Raw speech is full of restarts, hedges and half-abandoned sentences. Meram strips them, applies your dictionary, and matches the register to where you’re typing — before the text ever reaches the screen.

The disfluency pass on the left is computed in your browser, from that raw sentence, so you can see exactly what would be taken out. The rewrites on the right are recorded examples of what the model returns — this page holds no API key and does not transcribe anything.

Meram

Install it. Hold the key.

Give it a microphone and your own provider key. It is dictating.

  • No account, no sign-in, no product analytics
  • History, dictionary and settings live in a local SQLite file you can open
  • Your API key goes to the OS keystore, and only ever to the provider it belongs to
  • Uninstall and the only thing left is that folder — delete it and it’s gone

What Meram asks for

Microphone
Required. The stream opens when you hold the key and is released when you let go — it is not held open between dictations.
Accessibility (macOS)
Required there for typing into another application. Without it macOS will not let any app send a keystroke, and Meram says so rather than failing quietly.
Keyboard hook
Optional. Hold-to-talk needs a low-level hook; if it can’t load, Meram falls back to tap-to-toggle instead of pretending the key is being watched.

That’s the list. Screen context is a separate, off-by-default setting, and the page that explains exactly what leaves your machine is the same one that admits what does. Read the privacy page →

And the rest of it

Hold, or tap
Hold-to-talk by default, tap-to-toggle if you’d rather. One setting, and the fallback is automatic when the hook is unavailable.
Your own vocabulary
Terms mined from your own history — ranked by frequency, recency and the app you’re typing in — handed to Whisper as biasing. On device, at no storage cost.
Command mode
Select text, press Ctrl+Shift+R, and say what to do with it. Consoles are skipped deliberately: with no selection, Ctrl+C there is an interrupt.
Translate as you speak
Speak Turkish, land English. It is a setting on the dictation you are already doing, not a second tool to open.
History that is yours
Every dictation, searchable, in a local SQLite database. Delete one, delete all of them, or keep the audio only if you ask to.
Two providers
Groq by default, OpenAI if you prefer. On-device Whisper exists in the code and stays disabled until its packaged audio contract is proven.
Readable without motion
Every animation has a still equivalent, and the capsule’s state is legible without sound, colour or movement.
Windows now, macOS in progress
One codebase, one repository. macOS already dictates and types; it is unsigned, so it is not published yet.

On your machine.
No account. No analytics.

  • What leavesYour audio, to the provider you selected — plus the transcript when you turn on cleanup or translation. That is the whole list.
  • What staysHistory, dictionary, snippets and settings, in a SQLite file and a settings file on your disk that you can read and delete.
  • Your keyStored through the OS keystore where one is available, and sent only to the provider it belongs to. Never to us — we have no server for it to reach.
  • No product telemetryThere is no usage analytics in the app. Crash reporting exists only in builds compiled with a DSN, and that is not the default.

There is no fully offline path today, so this page does not claim one. On-device Whisper is written and deliberately disabled until it can be packaged, checksummed and verified — when that ships, this paragraph changes before the marketing does.

Do I need an API key?

Yes, today. Meram sends your audio to Groq or OpenAI with a key you supply; there is no hosted mode yet. Groq’s Whisper models cost roughly four cents per hour of audio, billed by Groq.

Does my voice leave my computer?

Yes — to the provider you chose, and nowhere else. There is no fully offline mode today. On-device Whisper exists in the code and is disabled until it can be packaged and verified.

Why does Windows warn me about the installer?

Because it isn’t signed. No Authenticode certificate exists for the project yet, so SmartScreen shows an unknown-publisher warning. More info → Run anyway.

Does it update itself?

Yes. An installed copy checks GitHub on launch and updates in the background. That channel is unsigned too, which is a deliberate beta trade-off and one that has to be closed before anything is called stable.

Is there a Mac version?

Not yet. macOS runs from the same codebase and already dictates and types, but it is neither signed nor notarized, so it is not published.

Will it stay free?

The beta is free. When hosted transcription ships there will be a paid plan and a free tier — and bringing your own key will stay the cheapest way to use it.
